\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\n###### Abstract\n\nReport issue for preceding element\nWith the popularity of large language models (LLMs), undesirable societal problems like misinformation production and academic misconduct have been more severe, making LLM-generated text detection now of unprecedented importance. Although existing methods have made remarkable progress, a new challenge posed by text from privately tuned LLMs remains underexplored. Users could easily possess private LLMs by fine-tuning an open-source one with private corpora, resulting in a significant performance drop of existing detectors in practice. To address this issue, we propose PhantomHunter, an LLM-generated text detector specialized for detecting text from unseen, privately-tuned LLMs. Its family-aware learning framework captures family-level traits shared across the base models and their derivatives, instead of memorizing individual characteristics. Experiments on data from LLaMA, Gemma, and Mistral families show its superiority over 7 baselines and 3 industrial services, with F1 scores of over 96%.\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n= Date: June 18, 2025\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\n1 Introduction\n--------------\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nLarge language models (LLMs) have successfully revolutionized the way people organize and produce text and inspire productivity in a wide range of applications Kaddour et al. ([2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib21)). However, undesirable societal problems caused by the misuses of LLMs also rapidly emerge, such as cheating in academic writing Koike et al. ([2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib22)), creating misinformation Chen and Shu ([2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib4)), and accelerating information pollution Puccetti et al. ([2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib32)). As the front-line barrier against such threats, automatic detection techniques of LLM-generated text (LLMGT) is now of unprecedented importance.\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nLLMGT detection generally distinguishes LLM-generated and human-written text via binary classification. Existing methods either learn common textual features (e.g., stylistic clues; Guo et al., [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib13)) shared across LLMs using representation learning or design distinguishable metrics between human and LLM texts based on LLMs’ internal signals (e.g., token probabilities;Shi et al., [2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib36)). For both categories, their tests were mostly conducted on data from publicly available LLMs, assuming that users generate text using public, off-the-shelf services. We argue that this situation is being changed due to the recent development of the open-source LLM community. With the help of platforms like HuggingFace 1 1 1[https://huggingface.co/models](https://huggingface.co/models) and the efficient LLM training techniques like low-rank adaptation (LoRA)Hu et al. ([2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib18)), building fine-tuned LLMs with customized private datasets has become much easier than before. For instance, there have been over 60k Llama-based derivative models on HuggingFace Meta AI ([2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib28)). After private fine-tuning on unknown corpus, the learned characteristics of base models could change and the LLMGT detectors would fail (will empirically show in Section[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#S2 "2 How Does Fine-tuning Affect LLMGT Detection? ‣ PhantomHunter: Detecting Unseen Privately-Tuned LLM-Generated Text via Family-Aware Learning")), shaping a new risk that malicious users can generate harmful texts privately without being caught by LLMGT detectors. A new challenge arises: How to detect text generated by privately-tuned open-source LLMs?\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nTo address this issue, we first conduct an analysis of differences between the base LLMs and its derivative models via fine-tuning on data of different topics. The result reveals that compared to other non-homologous models, the token probability lists of text on the fine-tuned LLM have a higher similarity to the probability lists on the base LLM, exhibiting clear “family trait”. Inspired by this finding, we propose to perform family-aware learning by extracting probabilistic features of base models and design PhantomHunter, a detector targeted at text generated by unseen privately-tuned LLMs. Our main contributions are as follows:\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n* •New Risk: We empirically expose the new risk that current LLM-generated text detectors may fail to detect text from privately-tuned LLMs.\n\nReport issue for preceding element \n* •New Method: We propose PhantomHunter, which trained via family-aware learning to learn common traits shared in typical open-source LLM families.\n\nReport issue for preceding element \n* •High Performance: Experiments on data from 3 LLM families show that PhantomHunter outperforms 7 baselines and 3 industrial services.\n\nReport issue for preceding element \n\n2 How Does Fine-tuning Affect LLMGT Detection?\n----------------------------------------------\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nPreliminary Experiment. To explore the extent to which fine-tuning affects the performance of LLM-generated text detectors, we fine-tuned LLaMA-2 7B using arXiv abstract corpus by saving checkpoints at different fine-tuning steps, which were then used to generated test samples. We experimented on two trainable detectors, RoBERTa (semantic-based; Guo et al., [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib13) and SeqXGPT (probability-based;Wang et al., [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#bib.bib41)). Figure[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 2 How Does Fine-tuning Affect LLMGT Detection? ‣ PhantomHunter: Detecting Unseen Privately-Tuned LLM-Generated Text via Family-Aware Learning") displays their decaying trends in accuracy as the fine-tuning corpus size increases. For the LLM fine-tuned on 11.3M tokens, SeqXGPT even encounters an astonishing accuracy drop of 41%.\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\n![Image 3: Refer to caption](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1/x1.png)\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nFigure 1: Detection accuracy for LLM-generated text with increasing amounts of fine-tuning data.\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nImpact of Fine-tuning on Features. We fine-tuned 3 open-source LLMs using 3 sets of corpus from different domains, and obtained the generated text of 12 LLMs including the original base LLM (see Section[4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#S4.SS1 "4.1 Dataset Construction ‣ 4 Experiment ‣ PhantomHunter: Detecting Unseen Privately-Tuned LLM-Generated Text via Family-Aware Learning") for details). We obtain their probability lists on each LLM, and calculate cosine similarity between the probability lists of each fine-tuned model and that of the base one. From Figure[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2 How Does Fine-tuning Affect LLMGT Detection? ‣ PhantomHunter: Detecting Unseen Privately-Tuned LLM-Generated Text via Family-Aware Learning"), we intuitively observe that the probability lists of LLMs from the same LLM are more similar to each other, and clearly different from those of the others, which shows significant “family trait”.\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\n![Image 4: Refer to caption](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1/x2.png)\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nFigure 2: Cosine similarity between the token probability lists of fine-tuned models and those of the base ones. Darker colors indicate higher similarity.\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nInspiration. This observation reveals that the probability lists of the base models could be useful for modeling unseen fine-tuned ones. However, such “family traits” are not fully exploited by existing detectors because they treat all LLMs equally and may only learn the commonality across all involved LLMs (regarding the 12×\\times×12 area in the heatmap), not the family-level commonality (regarding each 4×\\times×4 areas), shaping a looser boundary against human text. Targetedly, we design PhantomHunter to learn such traits to address this issue.\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\n3 Proposed Method: PhantomHunter\n--------------------------------\n\nReport issue for preceding element\n\nPhantomHunter is a family-aware learning-based detector for unseen privately-tuned LLM-generated texts. The key insight behind PhantomHunter is to model the inherent family-level features shared between base LLMs and their fine-tuned descendants. Building on these observations, PhantomHunter employs a family-aware learning approach to detect texts from unseen privately-tuned LLMs. As illustrated in Figure[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15683v1#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3 Proposed Method: PhantomHunter ‣ PhantomHunter: Detecting Unseen Privately-Tuned LLM-Generated Text via Family-Aware Learning"), the architecture consists of three main components: 1) a base probability feature extractor, 2) a contrastive learning-based family encoder, and 3) a mixture-of-experts detection module.
